## 1. Bounded AR arms, and why I tried them

The neural models over-forecast because nothing in their input tells them a customer has
stopped buying. The obvious fix is to feed the customer's own history — recency,
frequency, tenure, i.e. Pareto/NBD's sufficient statistic. It made things clearly
**worse** (on the synthetic grid the LSTM's median |bias| went from 56% to 261%): those
counters keep growing through the 52-week rollout, so by mid-holdout the model is reading
values it never saw while fitting.

So I kept the same information and only changed the encoding, so that no holdout value
can leave the calibration range:

- **bounded32** — binary flags `active in the last 2 / 4 / 8 / 16 / 32 weeks` plus
  `has transacted before`; they saturate instead of drifting.
- **ratio** — recency/tenure, transaction rate, saturating tenure; bounded by
  construction.

Both encode exactly the same history as the unbounded version. That is the point: the
information is right, the unbounded encoding is unusable.

What they buy (table below): **the flags protect the level, the ratio triple protects the
ranking**. Ratio is the first neural configuration here to reach Pareto/NBD's
per-customer ranking (CDNOW 0.44 vs 0.45, gift 0.39 vs 0.38, electronics 0.31 vs 0.30).
Combining both keeps the ranking but not the level, so it wins no panel. Extending
calibration from two to three years improves the ranking on every panel and every
encoding (e.g. gift 0.33–0.39 → 0.42–0.44) and makes the encodings converge on
electronics.

Everything else is held fixed: all of these runs use **Valendin's embedder** — the
published one, where each feature keeps its own sqrt(cardinality)+1 vector — pinned, not
searched, in all 2,800 stored studies (LSTM and Transformer alike). Our own projected
embedder is never used here, so the only thing that differs between the rows below is the
AR channel.

## 2. A likely problem: the Optuna search does not select the best forecaster

Each replication searches 100 trials and keeps the trial with the lowest **teacher-forced
validation cross-entropy**. But the number I report comes from a **52-week rollout**.
Scoring all 2,880 stored forecasts against their own search logs says the two are barely
related:

- The winning validation loss hardly varies between replications (CV 0.08–3.1%), and the
  median run has 1–22 other trials within 0.5% of the winner — the objective is flat
  exactly where the choice is made.
- Rank correlation between a run's validation loss and its holdout |bias| is within ±0.25
  in 31 of 32 cells.
- On multichannel the selected trial had stopped at epoch ≤ 3 in 22–55% of runs, i.e.
  barely trained models being selected.

So a good part of the spread I report as ± sd is **selection noise, not model capacity**,
and more trials will not narrow it. I am now re-scoring the non-winning trials of archived
studies through the full forecast path to measure how much is lost this way. A first probe
gives a caveat: refitting the *same* checkpoint twice moved holdout bias by 16 points, so
the noise floor has to be measured before two trials can be compared at all. If this
confirms, the fix is to select on rollout quality rather than on validation loss, or to
score the ensemble of replications instead of their mean.
