# Ledger: kill / keep / refactor, all of `src/panelclv/`

Produced by ticket 05 (`issues/05-reachability-ledger.md`) by merging the three lane
audits (02, 03, 04) with the golden run's reachability trace. **These verdicts are
proposals. Ticket 06 decides.**

Two files, one dataset:

- **`ledger.csv`** — the rows, machine-sortable: `lane, module, symbol, kind, lines,
  verdict, evidence, callers_src, callers_live, callers_dead_scripts, callers_oneshot,
  callers_tests, traced, audit`. Sort or count on `verdict`, `lane` or `audit`.
- **`ledger.md`** (this file) — the same rows rendered per subpackage, plus what only
  the merge can see: the cross-lane duplication list, the conflicts between audits,
  and where the three audits applied the dead-code rule inconsistently.

Both are generated from one table, so they cannot disagree.

## Method

Every module-level public symbol in `src/panelclv/` was enumerated by AST (plus the
public methods of `PanelConfig`, `ModelSpec`, `StudySuiteConfig`, `ForecastRun`,
`FitResult` and `Embedder`, and the five `mc_*` aliases in `models/__init__.py`;
`forward`/`__init__` are folded into their class row). For each, every reference in
every file was classified by AST as *import*, *call*, *attribute read* or *bare name*,
and the files were kept in five separate populations: `src/panelclv/`, the **live entry
points**, the **two broken plot scripts**, the **one-shot scripts**, and `tests/`. That
mechanical pass is what the caller columns hold; the verdicts and evidence come from the
audits, with the audit named in the `audit` column (`none` = no audit ruled, see below).

**Live entry points** (the map's corrected list): `scripts/run_studies.py`,
`scripts/validate_pareto_benchmark.py`, `scripts/validate_valendin_lstm.py`, and the
four live notebooks. `scripts/main_plot.py` and `scripts/main_plot_covar.py` are **not**
live — both are broken (`ValueError` / `TypeError`), so they keep nothing alive; where a
symbol's fate rests on them the verdict is `conditional-10`. Tests and one-shot scripts
are not callers. The thesis carve-out overrides all of it.

## Verdict vocabulary

| verdict | meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `keep` | live, and no structural defect the audits flagged |
| `refactor` | live, but a named defect: over-exposure, duplication, misplaced seam, dead option, or wrong layer |
| `kill` | no caller by the map's rule and no carve-out — deletable as it stands |
| `kill-candidate` | kill evidence exists but something complicates it (a test, a notebook import, an unmet convention); ticket 06 must choose |
| `conditional-10` | aliveness depends on what ticket 10 does with the two broken plot scripts |

No row reads `no-verdict`: for the eleven public symbols no audit mentioned, the row
carries a mechanically-derived verdict and its `audit` column reads `none`, so ticket 06
can see exactly which rows are lane-audit evidence and which are this merge's own.
Those eleven are `PanelConfig.from_dict`, `ARFeatureState`, `load_pnbd_dataset`,
`ProjectedEmbedder`, `experiments…DataBuilder`, `tuning…DataBuilder`,
`LSTM_SEARCH_DEFAULTS`, `TRANSFORMER_SEARCH_DEFAULTS`, `ModelSpec.is_neural`,
`create_suite_root`, `DEFAULT_METRICS` — and one of them (`DataBuilder`) turned out to be
a duplication both audits missed (D1).

## Conflicts between audits

The highest-value output of the merge: places where two audits propose incompatible
fates, or where one audit's aliveness argument rests on something another audit
demolished. Ticket 06 should read these before the tables.

**C1 — `forecast_from_checkpoint` and the stepper ruling. Audit 03's key witness does
not exist.** Audit 03 makes "a live entry point already takes the silent branch" its
evidence #3 for the stepper question and concludes that option (B) (make the pairing
checkable) "is the only option that closes the demonstrated `main_plot.py` bug at a cost
measured in lines rather than in notebook edits". Audit 04 shows `main_plot.py` cannot
run at all (1-D aggregate into `metrics_table` → `ValueError`). **Verified further here by running it —
`PYTHONPATH=src python scripts/main_plot.py` raises `NotImplementedError` at `:277` in the
`_load_dataset` stub** — so execution never reaches the forecast at `:119`/`:155`, let
alone the `metrics_table` line.** The miscrossing is real in the code and unreachable in
practice. Consequence for ticket 06: (B) keeps its structural argument (the pairing is
written 3× and enforced 0×, and `plot_utils` + the notebooks *can* miscross) but loses
its live-bug argument, and the same reasoning removes the last runnable caller of
`forecast_from_checkpoint`, so "fix it" and "delete it" are now both on the table where
audit 03 assumed only "fix it".

**C2 — is `compute_forecast_metrics` the single scoring authority? The two audits
answer differently.** Audit 03 treats the authority claim as true and concludes that the
`mape_aggregate_style` / `mape_aggregate` mismatch means "the doc is what is wrong, not
the code". Audit 04 shows the claim is false package-wide: `tuning` recomputes all three
numbers and two disagree (D8). Merged: CLAUDE.md's metrics paragraph needs **two**
corrections, not one — the key name *and* either an explicit `tuning` carve-out or
`weekly_aggregate_rollout_metrics` renamed off the shared vocabulary. Ticket 08's input.

**C3 — `save_predictions_to_csv`'s caller count is overstated by audit 04's own
rule.** Audit 04 calls it "pure prediction I/O used by 6 modules and 5 entry points"
while its own §1 establishes that notebook *imports* are not calls and that both plot
scripts are dead. Verified by AST: the three live-notebook references are imports never
called, and the two calling scripts are the dead ones — so its **live entry-point call
count is 0**, and it is alive entirely through three live `src/` callers
(`monte_carlo_forecasting:324`, `runner:137/205`, `analysis:212`) plus the dead
`forecast_run`. This does not change its verdict (`refactor`: move it out of the
plotting module) but ticket 06 should not quote "5 entry points" as the reason it
matters.

**C4 — `compute_class_weights`: audit 03 is split against itself, and against the
map.** Its §1 rules the function "live callers, no live consumer"; its conclusion says
"the same verdict [kill] extends to `compute_class_weights`". But two live notebooks
*call* it (verified), `tests/test_losses.py` is dedicated to it, and killing it edits two
live notebooks in the same commit under the map's notebook rule. It is only dead in the
sense that the CE branch discards its output — i.e. it is dead *because* `weighted_ce`
is dead. Recorded as `kill-candidate`, and the choice is really one choice with
`FocalLoss`/`SquaredEMDLoss`/`focal_gamma`/`weighted_ce`: kill the whole loss-variant
cluster or keep it whole.

**C5 — `scripts/validate_valendin_lstm.py` is simultaneously duplication to remove
(audit 02) and a floor gate that must not move (audit 03/the map).** Audit 02 records
three re-implementations inside it (its own `WEEKS_PER_YEAR = 52`, its own cohort filter,
its own `dayofyear // 7` week index) as duplication D3/D7; the map makes the same script
one of two executable definitions of benchmark fidelity. Not a contradiction, but every
dedup ticket touching D2/D3/D7 must re-run the two validate scripts and land in their
bands, and the script's own copies may be *deliberate* insulation from the package.
Ticket 06 should state which reading it takes.

**C6 — the "no caller outside its own module" prong was applied at two different
granularities.** Audit 02 records `panel_config.normalize_embedded_cols`,
`PanelConfig.data_config` and `PanelConfig.schema` as having "external callers: none".
All three are used from `data_preparation/dynamic_panel_dataset.py` — a different module
in a different subpackage (`:76` imports `normalize_embedded_cols`; `:605` reads
`.schema`; `:617` reads `.data_config`). Audits 03 and 04 applied the prong per *module*
and labelled lane-internal cases explicitly ("`training_utils` (lane-internal) only").
So the data lane's over-exposure table overstates by three rows: those three are the
`configs` → `data_preparation` contract, not private-in-practice helpers. This ledger
records them as `refactor` for their real defects, not as over-exposure.

**C7 — one-shot scripts counted as callers in audit 02, explicitly excluded in audit
03.** Audit 02 argues `observed_past` is always `()` by citing
"every live caller" including `scripts/verify_transformer_training.py:44`; audit 03
writes "`FitResult.history` is read only by `scripts/verify_transformer_training.py` — a

`rollout_horizon` mention that is really a pre-package traceback, and
`Pareto_Datasets`' import-only `compute_class_weights`) but its liveness tables do not
carry the distinction. One concrete stale row: audit 03 says
